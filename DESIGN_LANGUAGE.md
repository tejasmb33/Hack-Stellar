# DESIGN_LANGUAGE.md
## Mini-Hackathon Event Website — Complete Visual Design & Experience Contract

**Status:** Finalized creative direction; organizer-specific content and brand assets still need approval.

**Priority:** Finish the complete, polished, usable website first. The cinematic **Start Your Journey** experience is an **optional final phase** and must never compromise the quality of the core site.

> **North star:** **Cinematic wonder, immediate clarity.** Evoke the scale, mystery, atmosphere, and gravitational beauty associated with *Interstellar*: deep space, Earth’s atmospheric horizon, sparse stars, deep blues, monumental typography, and continuous, meaningful movement. The result must still be an exceptionally clear hackathon information and registration website, not a visual-effects demo.

This document specifies **visual language, information hierarchy, layouts, imagery, motion, interactions, and responsive behavior only**. It does **not** prescribe a technology stack, frameworks, libraries, plugins, 3D software, hosting, infrastructure, or coding approach. All creative descriptions are intended as observable design outcomes, not technical instructions.

---

## 0. Mandatory organizer intake — ask before creating the website

The website creator **must request** the following rather than guess, generate placeholder facts, or begin with an invented event identity. Ask for missing items in one organized round. Mark unpublished or unconfirmed information clearly; never show guesses as official event details.

### Identity and registration
- [ ] Exact **event name**, official short name, tagline if any, and organizing club name.
- [ ] **Club logo** (mandatory and visible in the **top-left of the opening screen**) and **event logo** if one exists (original, preferably transparent/high-resolution), brand usage restrictions, and any existing official color rules.
- [ ] Event **date, venue, timing, city, eligibility, team size**, organizer contacts, and official social links.
- [ ] Exact **prize pool**, prize split (if public), and approved wording/currency formatting.
- [ ] Working **Google Form registration URL**, registration deadline, registration-open/closed status, and any important eligibility notes. Both primary and floating registration buttons must use the approved link.
- [ ] A one- or two-sentence official event description.

### Companies and people — three separate groups
- [ ] **Problem statements from:** verified list of contributing companies/organizations, exact display names, approved logos, intended order, and whether attribution is public.
- [ ] **Judges from:** verified organizations/affiliations, exact display names, approved logos, intended order, and whether attribution is public.
- [ ] **Sponsors:** verified sponsor names, tiers (if applicable), approved logos, relative prominence/contractual placement, and permission to publish.
- [ ] For **each confirmed judge**: full name, role/title, affiliated organization, **LinkedIn profile URL**, and authorized portrait if available. Confirm that each judge and affiliation can be announced publicly.
- [ ] Confirm whether any company appears in more than one group. Keep **each role separately labeled**; do not treat a problem provider, employer of a judge, and sponsor as interchangeable.

### Event mechanics and participant guidance
- [ ] Exact **event flow**, including what happens **before** the event and the agenda **on the event day**. Confirm what is announced vs still tentative.
- [ ] Organizer-approved wording of the key rule: **participants present on the event day; there is no on-site building phase**. Confirm what must be prepared beforehand.
- [ ] What participants should build/prepare, expected output or deliverable, whether there are rounds, presentation length, submission requirements, eligibility, and important deadlines.
- [ ] Plain-language **judging criteria** or emphasis, without requiring a detailed scoring sheet.
- [ ] FAQ answers, rules, and contact details.

### Problem statement collection
- [ ] Up to **30 numbered problem statements** with official title, **full brief**, short one-sentence summary, originating company, any categories/tags, constraints, and any supporting links or resources. Approve the preview text independently from the full brief.
- [ ] Confirm whether statements are all public at launch; if not, supply disclosure dates and approved coming-soon wording.
- [ ] Confirm whether participants can choose any statement or are assigned one, and whether selection is required during registration.

### Brand-asset integrity
- Use supplied **official** marks when available. If a company logo is missing, **request permission to source an official public logo/brand asset** and verify its accuracy before publishing.
- If no approved asset can be obtained, use a carefully typeset **text-only company name** in the same visual container. **Do not fabricate or AI-recreate corporate logos** and do not imply an unconfirmed sponsorship or affiliation.
- Do not crop, recolor, stretch, skew, place gradients inside, or place decorative effects over a company's actual mark unless its brand guidelines explicitly allow it.

**Intake outcome:** create a content inventory with confirmed, pending, and not-for-publication items. Use the approved information to populate the sections below; do not invent facts to fill layout gaps.

## 1. Signature creative direction

**Working style name:** **Interstellar Atmosphere / Dark Cosmic Minimalism**.

The visual atmosphere should convey:

- **Immensity:** generous negative space, restrained compositions, and large confident headings.
- **Depth:** near-black space, layered midnight-blue nebula haze, soft volumetric blue illumination, and occasionally a warm edge-light reminiscent of an accretion disk.
- **Credibility:** clear, quiet information design and professional corporate-logo presentation.
- **Purpose:** visitors immediately understand **what the event is, the prize pool, how to register, what happens on the day, and what the problems are**.

### Visual interpretation of the supplied references

1. **Earth-horizon opening reference (latest supplied screenshot):** the **primary hero composition reference**: a huge crisp event title against black space and fine stars, a tasteful blue atmospheric crescent, and the curvature of Earth across the lower part of the frame. Add the club logo to the upper left and preserve a clear space for event details, prize, and registration. The supplied sample’s unfamiliar lettering is a **layout reference only**, never the event’s title or typeface.
2. **Blue nebula / atmospheric-depth reference:** the **primary mood and lighting reference**. Borrow deep blue cosmic haze, subtle volume, cinematic scale, and a beautiful distinction between near and far layers.
3. **“Explore Space” / Earth visual reference:** borrow refined headline proportions, restraint, generous negative space, a prominent central action, and balanced hierarchy.
4. **Purple flowing-ribbon reference:** borrow only the sense of sculpted dimensional curves, smooth continuity, and elegant 3D form. **Reject** its pink/purple-dominant palette.

### Cosmic imagery rules

- **Preferred:** nebulae, drifting blue gas/cloud forms, dark gravitational lensing, a subtle black-hole silhouette or accretion ring, atmospheric light trails, distant stars used sparingly.
- **Signature opening motif:** a realistically lit **Earth horizon / upper-atmosphere perspective** occupying the lower portion of the hero, inspired by the most recent screenshot; Earth may be partially hidden by darkness and should feel enormous rather than illustrated.
- **Secondary motifs:** blue nebula plumes, rare distant stars, flowing gravitational arcs, softly luminous dust, and a black-hole-inspired form in later transitions if it helps the narrative.
- **Avoid:** solar-system lineups, multiple cartoon planets, sci-fi spaceship dashboards, busy starfields, generic galaxy wallpapers, exaggerated neon cyberpunk, obvious stock-asset collages, and constantly switching between unrelated cosmic subjects.
- Evoke the **feeling** of *Interstellar*, not literal movie frames, title treatments, copyrighted stills, or recreated promotional art.

**Hierarchy rule:** atmospheric media serves the content; it never reduces legibility or forces users to work to find information. **The first screen must look complete and exceptional even if the optional journey is never created.**

## 2. Color system

| Role | Suggested color | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Void black | `#05080E` | Main background, deepest hero edges |
| Deep midnight navy | `#0A1422` | Section alternation, content surfaces |
| Cosmic blue | `#12304A` | Nebula shadows, broad atmospheric gradients |
| Electric blue | `#3D9BFF` | Primary CTA emphasis, selected states |
| Ice cyan | `#80D8FF` | Small highlights, luminous edges |

- Most of the page should stay dark and low saturation. Blue illumination should be **localized**, not a glow applied to everything.
- Reserve vivid highlights for primary actions and selected states. Purple may occur only as a faint natural nebular nuance; never as the visual identity.
- Cards should have controlled surface separation through thin low-contrast borders, soft navy elevations, or slight transparency—not opaque gray rectangles.
- Corporate logos retain their approved colors; place them in restrained contrast-safe frames so they feel integrated without being altered.

## 3. Typography and editorial voice

### Visual typography

- One expressive **modern geometric or neo-grotesque sans-serif** voice for headings and UI. A restrained technical/monospaced accent may be used **only** for problem numbers, timings, section indices, or other small metadata.
- Hero event name: commanding, carefully wrapped, approximately **64–104 px on large desktop**, scaling gracefully by viewport and name length.
- Section titles: approximately **40–64 px on desktop**; mobile titles approximately **30–40 px**.
- Body text: approximately **16–18 px**, roomy line height; long problem descriptions should be particularly comfortable to read.
- Company labels and section eyebrows: clean, modest, strongly legible; avoid tiny uppercase labels for essential content.
- Prefer one or two type weights per component. Establish hierarchy by size, spacing, and contrast rather than constant neon emphasis.

### Copywriting character

**Confident, precise, cinematic but not cryptic.** A visitor should not need to decode thematic metaphors to understand a real event instruction.

Good functional labels: **Register Now**, **Problem Statements**, **Event Flow**, **What to Prepare**, **How Judging Works**, **Meet the Judges**, **View Full Brief**.

Avoid replacing them with vague labels such as “Enter the Universe” or “Discover Your Destiny.” A poetic line may accompany a clear label, never replace it.

## 4. Site structure and first-scroll choreography

The page must feel like a deliberate cinematic sequence **without forced full-page snap scrolling**. The organizer's preferred reading order is mandatory:

1. **Opening hero:** event name, prize pool, primary **Register Now** action, minimum essential event metadata.
2. **Affiliations showcase:** **Problem Statements From → Judges From → Sponsors**, in **that exact order**.
3. **Event flow:** how the event works and what happens before/on the day.
4. **Problem statements:** browse up to 30 real briefs.
5. **What to prepare / how judging works:** if not fully incorporated in the event-flow section, make these immediately discoverable and clearly linked.
6. **Named judges:** portraits or elegant identity cards with verified names, affiliations, and LinkedIn links.
7. **FAQ, final registration reminder, organizers/contact, and footer.**

### Approximate desktop visual rhythm

- **Viewport 1:** complete hero with obvious prize and registration CTA.
- **Next natural scroll:** company affiliations showcase, integrated visually rather than as three unrelated logo screenshots.
- **Following natural scroll:** event-flow content begins.
- This is a **design target, not rigid screen snapping**. On phones and smaller laptops, allow content to occupy the vertical space it needs; never shrink logos or text simply to force exact scroll counts.

### Navigation

- Quiet, unobtrusive top navigation with **club logo at the top-left**, plus **Event Flow**, **Problems**, **Judges**, and **Register** shortcuts.
- The club logo should feel authoritative, not buried under cosmic effects. An event logo may sit beside it or within the hero if branding permits.
- Navigation stays accessible as the visitor moves through a long page. It must not dominate the first frame or obscure section titles.
- Simple, predictable navigation takes priority over unusual cinematic menus.

## 5. Section-by-section visual specification

### 5.1 Hero — “the invitation” / Earth's edge

**This is the centerpiece of the whole website.** Design it to an excellent finished standard **before thinking about an optional cinematic intro**. The aesthetic must not be compromised to make a later journey easier.

**The intended first frame on a laptop:**

- A carefully art-directed **near-black upper-space sky**, with sparse, finely resolved stars and restrained blue nebular depth.
- A **large curved Earth horizon across the lower third to lower half** of the viewport, visible from space. The edge has a delicate cyan/ice-blue atmospheric glow; surface detail is subdued and realistic, not a brightly colored classroom globe.
- **Club logo at the upper-left corner**, integrated into a calm navigation bar, with generous clear space. No oversized decorative badges competing with the mark.
- The **official event name is huge and immediately recognizable**, ideally in the upper-middle / central negative space. It is the visual hero, not the globe or a special effect. Line breaks must respond intelligently to the actual name.
- Beneath the name: the **real approved prize pool**, an obvious, high-contrast **Register Now** CTA, and only the minimum practical context (such as official date and venue when approved).
- A small, subtle visual affordance for continuing down the page, such as a directional cue or short **Explore the Event** label. This must not compete with registration.
- An **optional, secondary** control labeled **Start Your Journey** may be introduced **only if** the optional journey has actually been completed; never place a nonfunctional teaser button in the mandatory experience.

**Visual priority:** `Event name → Prize pool → Register Now → Essential date/venue → secondary exploration`.

**Text density:** the hero is not a paragraph. Use short labels, spacious composition, and visual grouping. Place longer explanations later.

**Earth placement and polish:**

- The atmospheric edge may sweep across the bottom in a broad, shallow arc, with a dark side fading into the page. It should suggest extreme scale and serenity.
- Keep a dark enough negative-space pocket behind the title and actions; never overlay busy clouds, high-detail landforms, or bright rim lighting behind essential letters.
- The camera viewpoint should feel anchored in the upper atmosphere or low orbit, **not** a catalog view of an entire floating planet.
- Preserve the screenshot's broad composition, but do not imitate its title lettering or omit hackathon necessities merely to match the reference exactly.
- The image must crop gracefully on mobile: retain a recognizable atmospheric arc without pushing the CTA below an unnecessary wall of scenery.

**On ordinary site entry:** the complete hero is available immediately. A very brief, nonblocking atmospheric/text settle is acceptable, but there must be **no mandatory intro, no long wait, and no dependency on the optional journey**. The elaborate staged appearance after a cinematic arrival is specified separately in the optional final phase.

**On scroll:** the curved Earth form may recede slightly, its rim-light may shift, and the starfield may gain depth. The content moves naturally upward as the scene hands off to the affiliation showcase. Avoid obvious model spinning or excessive camera shaking.

**Do not:** autoplay a long experience, gate the page behind movement, make Earth brighter than the event name, bury the prize amount, clutter the sky with multiple planets, or make the hero look unfinished unless the journey is built.

### 5.2 Affiliations showcase — “credibility in orbit”

**Required group order:**
1. **Problem Statements From**
2. **Judges From**
3. **Sponsors**

These are **three clearly titled, separate groupings** inside one visually continuous section. The section should read like a carefully art-directed set of constellations, **not a pasted logo board**.

**Logo treatment:**
- Use a consistent system of subtle frames/plates, balanced padding, optical sizing, and aligned baselines.
- Background plates may be translucent ink/navy with thin edge-light and restrained depth.
- Company logos should feel **embedded in the same atmosphere** as the page, while remaining crisp, accurate, and easy to recognize.
- Preserve each organization's clear-space and aspect-ratio rules. Bright or dark marks may need an approved light/dark asset or contrasting plate.
- Allow flexible wrapping for varying counts. Never make visitors chase unreadable moving logos.
- On desktop, groups may form layered rows with a restrained orbital flow; on mobile, use readable wraps or cards without overflow traps.

**3D opportunity:** one **shared** slow-moving depth field or curved orbital light plane **behind** the three groups. It may create a sense of moving through a cosmic gallery as the page scrolls. The actual logo assets stay mostly frontal and legible—**do not spin, flip, warp, or orbit logos so that names become hard to read**.

**Affiliation accuracy:** role labels must be explicit. A judge's employer is not automatically a sponsor; a problem provider is not automatically a judge.

### 5.3 Event flow — “clarity after spectacle”

**Visual energy reduces here.** After the immersive affiliation area, transition to a darker, quieter information zone with strong typography.

The section must make the core event rule unmissable:

> **Event day is for presenting your prepared solution—not building on-site.**

Use this wording only after organizer approval; do not imply what participants must prepare until confirmed.

**Recommended visual structure:** a large **Event Flow** heading, one-line event summary, then a numbered vertical sequence with generous spacing and an elegant connecting line or illuminated nodes.

- Differentiate **Before the Event** and **On Event Day** clearly.
- Steps should use real, organizer-approved sequence and times—no invented phases or judging rounds.
- Put actionable instructions (“Choose a statement,” “Prepare materials,” “Present your work”) in concise, scannable cards **only where confirmed**.
- Add adjacent clear sub-panels for **What to Prepare**, **What Happens on the Day**, and **How Judging Works**; judging criteria are described in plain language, not as a dense scoring spreadsheet.
- Give readers enough contrast and stillness to understand expectations on the first pass.

**Motion:** modest step reveals and a quiet progress line are enough. No sweeping 3D shapes through paragraph text, and no scroll hijacking.

### 5.4 Problem statements — “the essential destination”

This is the **most information-dense and task-critical section**. It must look unmistakably like a collection of official event **problem statements**.

**Title:** **Problem Statements** with a clear count, e.g., “30 statements” when the actual published count is confirmed. Add a one-sentence guide to selection/preparation using only confirmed rules.

**Primary browsing pattern:** **responsive searchable, filterable card grid**—not a horizontally scrolling wall.

- Desktop: typically **three columns** at comfortable widths; two columns on intermediate screens; **one column on phones**.
- Always show **problem number** (e.g., `PS–01`), **official title**, **contributing organization/name**, and **short summary**.
- Optionally show meaningful, organizer-approved category tags. Avoid inventing categories.
- Include a visible **Search problem statements** field and filters for real categories/company only when the data supports them; a clear reset state; and understandable result counts.
- Full statement should be opened through an obvious **View Full Brief** interaction that retains reading context: an accessible expanded panel, drawer, or dedicated detail view. Long descriptions must never be forced into tiny cards.
- The expanded view presents the **complete exact approved statement**, attribution, constraints, relevant links, and a clear close/back action.
- On mobile, make titles and company names readable without truncating the essential meaning; filters must not dominate the screen.

**Visual treatment:** restrained dark-blue cards; thin luminous edging on hover/focus; generous white space; subtle numerical indexing; firm headline hierarchy. Atmosphere can appear *between* card groups but not behind paragraphs at high contrast.

**Important interaction states:** default, hover, focus, selected filter, loading/awaiting publication if relevant, no results, and full statement open.

**Never:** use 30 forced horizontal slides, auto-advance statements, hover-only disclosure, a 3D carousel that hides text, or truncated briefs with no clear way to read the complete version.

### 5.5 Judges — “real people, real credibility”

Present actual **judge names**, **role/title**, **affiliation**, and an explicit **LinkedIn** profile action for each verified judge.

- Clean portrait cards if approved photographs exist; otherwise use tasteful typographic/initial-based identity cards.
- Keep cards visually coherent with company plates, but **people and LinkedIn links** deserve distinct emphasis.
- Use clearly readable full names and company identification; never force essential information into a hover reveal.
- LinkedIn should be labeled **LinkedIn** with a recognizable visual cue; external destination is clear.
- If an affiliation appears in the early logo showcase, the later judge card provides the person's identity and profile; the two sections should complement rather than duplicate each other.

### 5.6 FAQ and final conversion

- Use short, unambiguous questions: who may participate, team rules, what to prepare, whether building happens on the day, how presentations work, and registration deadline—**only with confirmed answers**.
- Use quiet collapsible groups or compact editorial blocks; never make participants guess the official rules.
- End with a strong but smaller **Register Now** prompt, event identity, contact, and links.
- The final section remains dark, polished, and calm; no second overwhelming cinematic climax is necessary.

## 6. Registration visibility and behavior

**The site has two complementary registration affordances:**

1. **Hero primary CTA:** a large visible **Register Now** button on initial entry; it opens the supplied Google Form directly.
2. **Floating CTA:** a bottom-right **Register Now** button that **does not appear while the primary hero CTA is visible**. It appears once the visitor scrolls past the hero CTA, and remains available through subsequent content sections.

### Floating CTA visual behavior

- Elegant blue-accent capsule with high-contrast text, visible focus, and a subtle entrance (fade/short upward translation); never a large pulsing beacon.
- Maintain sensible distance from edges and device safe areas; do not obstruct problem cards, filter controls, the full-brief close action, or important mobile UI.
- When the user scrolls back to the visible hero CTA, **hide the floating CTA** to avoid redundant calls to action.
- In a problem-detail view or dialog, the floating CTA must not obscure essential controls; it may temporarily recede if necessary.
- Both CTA destinations must be the **same verified Google Form**, unless the organizer explicitly supplies a different approved action/status (e.g., registration closed).

## 7. Motion and 3D — “cinematic but controlled”

### Motion budget by section

| Area | Allowed intensity | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | **Medium** | Atmospheric depth, subtle gravitational light, strong opening impression |
| Three company groups | **Medium** | One coherent flowing 3D light/space element, slow parallax, transition between groups |
| Event flow | **Low** | Make steps easy to follow |
| Problem statement grid | **Very low** | Preserve scanning, search, reading, and comparison |
| Judge cards | **Low** | Polished hover/focus feedback only |
| Footer/FAQ | **Very low** | Maximum clarity |

### Allowed movement language

- Slow parallax in **background layers only**, subtle scroll-linked light movement, gentle reveals, slight card elevation/edge-light, soft transitions.
- Organic gravitational curves and subtle lensing are preferable to flashy rotations and constant mechanical motion.
- Keep noticeable background movement smooth and **slower than the reader's attention**; allow important content to sit still.
- As a guide: hover transitions around **150–250 ms**; section reveals around **400–800 ms**, with short distances and no stagger that delays reading.
- Motion must have a reason: guide the gaze, mark a section transition, or suggest scale; **not** decorate every object.

### Firm prohibitions

- No scroll hijacking, mandatory animation waits, content that becomes readable only after an effect, endless spin cycles, strobe/flicker, forced 3D object manipulation, autoplay sound, or motion that interrupts the act of reading.
- No animated background behind a long problem brief if it reduces concentration.
- No excessive particle counts, distracting cursor-following glows, neon borders on everything, or moving company logos that interfere with recognition.
- The complete experience must remain visually coherent with **motion reduced or disabled**. The visual hierarchy must not depend on movement to make sense.

**Decision rule:** if a 3D effect makes a statement, instruction, logo, or CTA slower to understand, remove or simplify the effect. **Information wins every time.**

### 7.1 The continuous scroll story — designed like a film, read like a website

The user requested **actual cinematic movement of visual elements while scrolling**, not merely a generic fade-in template. The page should feel like a single authored visual world whose **camera, atmosphere, light, foreground, and depth** change continuously as the visitor moves. This does **not** mean every card and word must move in 3D.

**Recommended visual storyboard (creative direction, not rigid timing):**

| Reading moment | Background / dimensional movement | Foreground / readable content |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Initial hero** | Huge dark Earth curvature below the frame; soft blue atmosphere; slow drifting stars; immense perceived distance. | Event title, prize, and primary registration appear stable and dominant. |
| **B. Leaving the hero** | Earth's rim seems to move lower or farther away as the point of view gently shifts; darkness opens into greater cosmic depth. | Hero content scrolls naturally away; the next section becomes legible without a hard visual cut. |
| **C. “Problem Statements From”** | A broad three-dimensional arc of blue light or gravitationally curved ribbon enters behind the company plates, slowly changing orientation as one passes the section. | Company identities are level, sharp, undistorted, and clearly grouped under their heading. |
| **D. “Judges From”** | The **same** evolving light form sweeps to another position or a nebula veil moves laterally; subtle depth makes the category change feel cinematic. | The label and its company logos are distinct; movement does not obscure affiliations. |
| **E. “Sponsors”** | The dimensional arc dissipates into soft blue particles or recedes behind the final grouping. The visual energy starts to quiet. | Sponsor marks are granted clear, respectful breathing room. |
| **F. Event Flow** | The cosmic field settles to a dark, calm background; a thin guiding line or constellation-like progression gently grows with the narrative. | Large step numbers, compact descriptions, and visual before/day-of separation are effortless to scan. |
| **G. Problem Statements** | A barely moving distant-space ambience remains around the perimeter; the visual world is continuous but unobtrusive. | Statement cards and detailed briefs are fully steady and readable. Filters and search have clear focus and active states. |
| **H. Judges / ending** | Very restrained halo, edge glow, or star depth returns as a closing visual echo of the opening sky. | Real people, affiliations, LinkedIn profiles, final registration, and contacts remain the focus. |

**Continuity contract:** transitions should seem motivated by the prior scene. One gravitational arc or blue-lit spatial field can evolve from section to section instead of introducing unrelated 3D assets at every scroll boundary. Avoid harsh changes in palette, bright flashes, random animations, or abrupt resets of perspective.

**Perceived dimensionality:** use gentle foreground/midground/background separation, meaningful occlusion behind decorative elements only, slight movement of light across curvature, and gradual movement in apparent camera perspective. The effect should read as cinema, **not** as a collection of spinning cards.

**Smoothness is part of design quality:** motion should be temporally coherent when scrolling up as well as down. A pause in scrolling should not make a hero or logo arrangement look broken or half-hidden. No content should require a particular scroll speed.

**Restraint by information density:** a complex ambient scene may persist behind the event flow and problems, but its visual contrast and speed must fade dramatically. Every important sentence, problem title, logo, and control should be understandable while standing completely still.

### 7.2 Micro-interaction and component behavior

- **Primary buttons:** a delicate luminous edge or fill transition; a clear pressed and focus state. Avoid glowing pulses that never stop.
- **Navigation links:** understated active-section indication and short spatial shifts on hover; avoid moving the whole header.
- **Company plates:** subtle rise, edge-light, or depth lift on hover; logos remain unchanged, crisp, and never rotate or deform.
- **Event timeline:** each step can reveal a concise icon/number and connecting trace as it enters, without delaying its text.
- **Problem cards:** a refined border/lighting change signals interaction; the clicked item opens its full approved brief in a stable, calm reading surface.
- **Judge profiles:** modest portrait/plate lighting, an unmistakable LinkedIn action, and no hover-only name or role.
- **Scroll cues:** minimal and functional; never occupy the central visual focus.
- **Interactive consistency:** feedback must look like it belongs to the same material and lighting system throughout the site.

## 8. Composition, grids, spacing, and surfaces

- Establish a consistent content width of roughly **1,180–1,320 px** on wide desktop, with generous side margins.
- Use a deliberate spacing rhythm: compact clusters inside cards, generous spacing between distinct sections, and especially generous breathing room in the hero.
- Desktop section spacing often around **96–144 px**; adapt on smaller screens to avoid both crowding and empty-scroll fatigue.
- Card corner radii should be modern and restrained (roughly **16–24 px**), with small consistent radii on controls.
- Dividers can be fine strokes or atmospheric fade-outs; do not isolate every section inside a glowing box.
- Use consistent alignment rules for titles, text columns, corporate marks, and CTA placements.
- One visual focal point per area: hero title, affiliations field, event timeline, problem grid, judge identities.
- Keep enough negative space to let glowing features look intentional rather than noisy.

---

## 9. Responsive behavior

### Large desktop / laptop (primary viewing context)
- Build a cinematic full-width first impression; keep hero text within a readable central/offset text zone.
- Keep event name, prize pool, and primary CTA obvious at initial viewing height.
- Present company groups in elegant rows and the problem list in a comfortably scannable grid.

### Tablet / smaller laptop
- Scale hero artwork and type proportionally. Let affiliations wrap rather than shrink excessively.
- Retain visible section labels and filters; reduce ornamental depth before reducing text size.

### Mobile (equally essential to successful registration)
- **Stack**, don't squeeze: a readable event title, prize, and registration first; affiliations in organized label-led wraps; a vertical event timeline; **one-column problem cards**.
- The affiliations area may take more than one viewport. Do not force the “two-scroll” desktop aspiration onto a phone.
- Keep interactive targets comfortably large (around **44 × 44 px minimum**) and spacing thumb-friendly.
- Place the floating CTA above any device-safe-area boundary and away from content or controls.
- Use far fewer decorative visual layers on mobile when necessary; preserve the recognizable atmospheric style.
- Ensure every piece of content available on desktop is accessible on mobile, including full problem briefs and judge LinkedIn actions.

---

## 10. Accessibility and informational trust

- Aim for **at least 4.5:1 text contrast** for normal text and clear contrast for interactive controls; decorative haze must not compromise this.
- Every key action remains identifiable without relying only on color, glow, or hover.
- Clear visible focus states; predictable reading order; readable body sizes; generous line spacing.
- Distinguish links, filters, and ordinary information by appearance and labels.
- Provide concise explanatory labels for logos and judge portraits; never embed important rules only inside artwork.
- Respect motion-reduction preferences and avoid flashes, strobing, and inaccessible 3D-only information.
- Do not imply official partnerships with unconfirmed logos or names.
- Validate official content, dates, prize figures, statement wording, judge identities, and external links with the organizer prior to publication.

## 11. Design quality checklist — mandatory acceptance criteria

The website's design is aligned **only if** all are true:

- [ ] It unmistakably feels like **dark, atmospheric, Interstellar-inspired cosmic minimalism**, not a purple neon template or a solar-system illustration.
- [ ] The hero makes **event name, prize pool, and Register Now** immediately apparent, with the **club logo top-left** and the **Earth-horizon/blue-atmosphere composition** polished as a finished standalone page.
- [ ] The initial company sequence is **Problem Statements From → Judges From → Sponsors** with accurate role labels.
- [ ] Company marks look professionally integrated into a shared visual system, never like pasted screenshots or manipulated logos.
- [ ] **Event Flow comes before Problem Statements**, and it clearly states **presentation on event day, not on-site building**.
- [ ] Up to **30 problem statements** are easy to scan; each has a clear number, title, attribution, summary, and access to the full brief; search/filter are clear and unobtrusive.
- [ ] Judges are identified by **actual full names and affiliations**, and have correct visible **LinkedIn** profile actions.
- [ ] A hero CTA is visible immediately. The bottom-right floating CTA appears **only after scrolling beyond it**, never obscuring critical content.
- [ ] **Actual cinematic scroll choreography** visibly links the hero and company sections via continuous Earth/nebula/gravitational depth and lighting; the instructions and problem-brief areas remain **calm**.

## 13. OPTIONAL LAST PHASE — “Start Your Journey” cinematic opening

> **Not part of core website acceptance. Do not begin this until the entire ordinary website has been designed, populated, polished, and reviewed on both laptop and mobile. If time or quality is limited, omit this phase entirely.**

### 13.1 Creative intent

This is an **optional one-time, button-triggered cinematic sequence**, **not** an autoplay experience and **not** a scroll-driven substitute for the website. Its purpose is to give visitors the sensation of traveling through an immense three-dimensional cosmic world **before arriving at the website's already-established visual atmosphere**.

The desired feeling: **quiet anticipation → overwhelming scale / graceful motion → arrival → atmosphere settles → interface emerges**.

This may be a visually rich illusion of 3D or a deeply dimensional scene; the success criterion is the **final viewer experience**, not a particular modeling method. A team may create the effect without organizer-supplied 3D assets if visual quality permits; this brief does not require any external models or prescribe tools. Do not present an unpolished effect as if it were cinematic simply because objects move.

### 13.2 Possible journey narratives — creative freedom is intentional

The journey is **not locked to Earth spinning**. Choose whichever story best matches the polished final homepage and can be executed well:

**Option A — Earth / Bengaluru arrival:** begin in the blackness above Earth, reveal the planet's slow curvature and blue atmosphere, rotate/reframe toward India, then Karnataka / Bengaluru in an abstract cinematic way. Do **not** require literal map accuracy or a city-level satellite zoom if that would break visual quality. The arrival can dissolve through atmospheric light and dark-blue particles into the landing scene.

**Option B — Interstellar flythrough:** travel through star fields, deep-blue nebula clouds, distant galaxies, or a single black-hole-inspired gravitational light ring. Objects should pass with believable scale, parallax, and restraint; avoid busy cartoon space objects. Gradually the field opens to reveal the exact space/atmosphere composition of the site.

**Option C — Hybrid (recommended if visually coherent):** start at the calm Earth horizon shown in the finished hero, pass through a short dark-blue spatial corridor / gravitational arc, and resolve back into the **same** Earth-horizon lighting and perspective. This keeps the signature imagery recognizable and reduces the risk of a jarring transition.

**Selection rule:** the completed ordinary site defines the destination. The cinematic story must bend to the homepage's existing art direction—not the other way around. **Never redesign or simplify an excellent first screen to accommodate this optional animation.**

### 13.3 Exact opening-to-arrival visual contract

The following sequence describes the visual result; it is intentionally independent of technical choices:

1. **Default ordinary visit (mandatory, even when the option exists):** the finished hero is immediately visible with club logo top-left, event name, prize, and **Register Now**. The secondary **Start Your Journey** button is small and clearly optional. The visitor can scroll or register without entering the journey.
2. **Visitor chooses Start Your Journey:** page controls and title withdraw softly while the present background remains visually anchored. The camera starts its authored one-time cinematic travel. No arbitrary loading wall or surprising automatic sound.
3. **Mid-journey:** the viewer experiences coherent depth, camera movement, scale, and celestial lighting. Motion is beautiful, intentional, and reasonably brief; there is no need to force every planet, nebula, and black hole into one sequence.
4. **Approach:** movement decelerates, particles/haze settle, and the scene gradually becomes the **same intended background, camera angle, color, and lighting as the actual website hero**. No jump to a separate graphic, unrelated wallpaper, or new design language.
5. **Arrival frame — critical:** **the background is already completely present first**. For a deliberate beat, it is allowed to stand on its own as a beautiful atmospheric composition. This frame should be visually indistinguishable from the final hero background of the ordinary site.
6. **Slow interface reveal — critical:** only *after arrival*, the club logo / masthead settles in, then the **event name**, approved **prize pool**, supporting date/venue, and finally the **Register Now** button **fade and rise in gracefully**. Keep delays short enough that the visitor is not stranded without a call to action; the CTA must be easy to discover immediately after the reveal. The secondary journey control may return quietly if replay is offered.
7. **Completion:** all site controls and normal scrolling are available; the page proceeds into **Problem Statements From → Judges From → Sponsors → Event Flow → Problem Statements** with exactly the same styling and behavior as the ordinary website. There must be **no visible cut, flash, color shift, or misalignment** at the end of the journey.

**The critical visual requirement:** **Journey end frame = real website background; website text and buttons appear afterward.** Do not render a mockup of the homepage in the animation and then switch visibly to a different homepage.

### 13.4 Interaction and pacing

- **Trigger:** a small secondary **Start Your Journey** control; the main **Register Now** CTA remains more prominent and usable before the journey.
- **Duration goal:** roughly **5–8 seconds** of authored travel, adjusted for quality. This is guidance, not a demand for artificially prolonged movement.
- **Skip:** visible, readable **Skip Journey** action throughout. Skipping lands smoothly on the fully usable actual site (do not delay the user with an additional long reveal).
- **Replay:** optional and deliberate; never replay automatically on refresh, navigation, or scrolling. If replay is offered, label it clearly and keep it secondary.
- **Reduced-motion / constrained-device visual alternative:** present the actual finished hero directly, with no forced travel. Nothing about event access or registration depends on the cinematic option.
- **No visual trickery with official information:** company identities, problem statements, judge data, and event rules must not be substituted with invented text during or after the sequence.
- **No audio requirement:** the experience must feel complete and cinematic in silence.
