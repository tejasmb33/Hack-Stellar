# INTERSTELLAR : THE BUILDATHON — Increase Cinematic 3D Motion

The new black-hole rendering looks significantly better than before. **Do not replace or redesign the improved black-hole model.**

However, there is still one major issue:

**The animation is too subtle. Unless I stare directly at the black hole and concentrate, I can barely tell that anything is moving.**

I want a much more dynamic, visually impressive, cinematic environment.

## 1. Make the Accretion Disk Visibly Move

The accretion disk needs more noticeable continuous motion.

- Increase the apparent speed of the glowing matter circulating around the black hole.
- Add clearly visible flowing filaments and luminous streaks.
- Introduce movement at different orbital speeds, with inner regions appearing faster than outer regions.
- Make the disk's turbulence and flowing energy visible without requiring the user to focus carefully.
- Preserve the realistic Gargantua-inspired appearance.

The disk should immediately communicate that enormous amounts of matter are circulating around the black hole.

Avoid making the entire disk spin like a rigid wheel.

## 2. Make Cosmic Dust More Dynamic

The surrounding environment still needs more visible activity.

Add or enhance:

- Clearly visible particles drifting across the foreground.
- Illuminated cosmic dust flowing near the accretion disk.
- Curved streams of small particles following orbital paths.
- Occasional brighter particles crossing the camera's field of view.
- Different particle speeds and depths to demonstrate genuine 3D parallax.
- Subtle trails on selected fast-moving particles.

The dust should be visible even when the visitor is not scrolling.

Avoid turning the scene into a chaotic meteor shower or covering the text.

## 3. Add Noticeable Cinematic Camera Movement

The camera currently feels too stationary.

Introduce slow but visibly perceptible camera movement while the visitor remains on the hero section.

For example:

- A gentle orbital camera drift around the black hole.
- Subtle changes in viewing angle.
- Natural perspective shifts.
- Foreground particles moving across the frame faster than distant stars.

The movement should make visitors feel like they are floating in space near an enormous black hole.

Keep the event title and interface stationary and readable.

Do not use constant screen shaking or excessive camera rotation.

## 4. Add Occasional Cinematic Moments

Introduce restrained, visually impressive moments that make the environment feel alive.

Examples:

- A luminous stream of matter moving through the accretion disk.
- A dense cluster of glowing cosmic dust passing through the foreground.
- A subtle gravitational distortion affecting nearby starlight.
- A brief change in the illumination of gas around the black hole.

These should emerge naturally from the simulation rather than appearing as random decorative animations.

Do not add explosions, flashing effects, or unrelated sci-fi objects.

The black hole must feel enormous, active, and powerful.

Use differences in particle movement, orbital flow, camera perspective, and depth to convey scale.

The visitor should immediately understand that this is a living 3D scene rather than static artwork.

Preserve the improved scroll-driven camera path.

Make its movement more noticeable while keeping transitions smooth.
